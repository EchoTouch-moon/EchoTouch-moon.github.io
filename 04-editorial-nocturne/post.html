The Craft of the Unfinished Note

Drafts that never close, wikis that breathe, and why a sentence left open is an invitation rather than a defect.

My notes folder contains four thousand files and perhaps three hundred conclusions. The ratio is not a failure of discipline; it is the product.1  The collection grows linearly; understanding arrives in bursts, like lightning counting itself in flash-units.

We treat completion as the terminal state of thought. A note is finished when it has a title, a summary, tags. But rereading says otherwise: the entries that keep teaching me are precisely the ones I left ragged — a quote without attribution, a question with three candidate answers circled, an instruction to future-me that begins “compare against…”

A closed notebook is inventory. An open one is weather.

Memory is a margin animal

Hypertext promised associative trails and delivered filing cabinets. The fix is not better taxonomies but wider margins: room for reaction adjacent to what provoked it. 2 This essay ships its own margins — narrow your browser window to watch the sidenotes fold back into the line.

Write less per note; revisit more often; link while warm.

A practice, stated plainly

  • Date every fragment; context outranks content within a decade.
  • Link before you file — a link made today survives reorganizations; a folder made today becomes tomorrow's archaeology.
  • End sessions mid-sentence when you can. The reopening is where thinking resumes fastest. 3 Ernest Hemingway claimed a version of this for novels; it works equally well for YAML.
